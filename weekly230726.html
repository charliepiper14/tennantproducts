The one line that matters: This was a new customer acquisition week wearing the costume of a soft revenue week. Store wide orders were flat at 195 against 196, but items per order fell from 2.42 to 1.77 and average order value fell 20.7 percent to $213.19. The Shopify data shows exactly why, and it is not the media. New customers rose 33.3 percent to 36 while returning customers fell 6.8 percent, and the prior week had been carrying a heavily discounted Digestive Enzyme run that sold 127 units against 46 this week. First orders are smaller than repeat orders, so a week that wins more new buyers will always show a lower average basket. Google returned 10.99x on $648.93, at a $16.93 cost per conversion, and even on Shopify's most conservative last click attribution it still reads 7.40x. The channel is performing. The constraint remains the $97.50 a day the campaigns are permitted to spend against $340 allocated.

The prior week was the anomaly, not this one. Between July 5 and 11 the store ran a heavily discounted Digestive Enzyme push, selling 127 units at an 18.5 percent discount rate against 46 units at 13.8 percent this week. That single product accounts for $7,628 of the $10,616 fall in gross sales, which is 71.9 percent of the entire decline from one item. Strip that promotional week out and the underlying business is stable. Google Ads value per conversion tells the identical story from the other side, $186.11 against $274.48, and the three week series shows weeks one and three within a dollar of each other.
The trade the week actually made. Nine more new customers and eleven fewer returning ones. A new customer typically buys one product to try the brand, a returning customer builds a basket. That is why acquisition weeks depress average order value and why average order value on its own is a poor scorecard for a channel whose stated job is new to brand growth. The right frame is cost to acquire against what the customer is worth over time, which is on slide 15. On that measure the week was strong, not weak.

Why the change of position. Last month's review recommended exact match negatives on tennantinstitute and the dr tenant misspelling on the basis that they were bleeding spend. This week's data does not support that. The dr tenant misspelling produced ten impressions, zero clicks and zero cost, so nothing is leaking, and it is a person looking for Dr Tennant who cannot spell the name. Tennant Institute is the clinic and the Shopify store handle, so a searcher using it is looking for the business. Negativing either would suppress brand demand rather than protect budget. The correct treatment is a low bid misspelling ad group inside Branded Search, which captures the intent at a fraction of the cost of the correctly spelled terms.
Two live cost issues worth a look. The navigational query tennantproducts com cost $19.41 for two clicks, a $9.71 cost per click on someone who has literally typed the website address into Google. Both are contained inside the ad approval and quality workstream rather than needing new budget.
Two keywords to review before the Iodine campaign is switched on. The campaign carries ki as a phrase match keyword, which is dangerously broad and will match a wide spread of unrelated queries the moment budget is applied. It also carries thyroid stimulating hormone on phrase match, which is an informational medical query rather than a purchase intent one, and it sits close to the disease claim line the brand deliberately stays behind. Both should be resolved before the campaign spends its first dollar, with ki either removed or tightly negatived and thyroid stimulating hormone either dropped or pointed at educational content that observes structure and function language only.
