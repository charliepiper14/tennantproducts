The LPV rate held below 5% even after the catalog override fix shipped May 4. Three layers were investigated and ruled out as the persistent cause: (1) Meta is no longer rewriting the destination URL, confirmed by John on May 11 when he clicked a live ABC ad and the captured URL pointed correctly to /pages/aw-yjrfsw with all UTM parameters intact. (2) Cloudflare Managed Challenge exclusions for paid LP paths are in place. (3) The Meta Pixel and CAPI are firing healthy server-side per John's May 13 audit (21.3K events in 28 days, Event Match Quality 6-8 of 10, zero diagnostic errors). The remaining cause sits where John landed it: the Consentmo GDPR cookie consent app was blocking all marketing and analytics tracking on first-time visitors via initialState 7 (ad_storage denied, analytics_storage denied, ad_user_data denied). Meta Pixel could not fire client-side until the visitor clicked Accept, meaning every cold ABC visitor was being counted as a click but not a Landing Page View by Meta's own measurement layer. John uninstalled Consentmo on May 12. This is post-period, and means the next reading window starting May 13 is the first clean opportunity to verify ABC LPV recovery into the 50% to 70% target band.
The underlying ABC product signal is genuinely positive when read on Shopify reality. ABC product units doubled week-over-week, from 6 to 12. Gross sales from ABC moved from $296.70 to $520.80, up 75.5%. While the product-attributable ROAS sits at 0.43x ($455 net divided by $1,049 spend), the unit demand response to two weeks of consistent ad delivery is the cleanest signal that the audience-product fit is real. CPC dropped from $0.94 to $0.76 and CTR rose from 1.36% to 1.54%, both indicators of an auction stabilising rather than fatiguing. Once the Consentmo block clears next week and LPV rate moves toward the target band, the small ABC purchase volume should compound into meaningful units sold weekly.
